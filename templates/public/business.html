Why Doing Business in Africa Still Feels Risky

African commerce has immense potential, but small businesses operate in high-friction environments where trust is scarce and verification tools were traditionally inaccessible.

Fake Transfer Slips & Reversed Funds

Fraudsters pose as wealthy buyers, send photorealistic fake banking screenshots, and pressure your sales team to dispatch goods before bank clearing, causing instant inventory loss.

Ghost Suppliers & Lost Deposits

SMEs looking for raw materials, electronics, or inventory pay 50% deposits to fabricated social vendors or fake CAC companies that vanish immediately after receiving payment.

Dispatch Riders & COD Divergence

Freelance delivery riders and untracked dispatch contractors steal high-value products or collect cash-on-delivery payments from customers and block contact numbers.

Recurrent Staff Theft & Document Forgery

Without formal employer registries, dismissed staff members who committed embezzlement simply change location and present forged CVs or fake guarantors to victimize another unsuspecting business.

Cross-Border Trade Friction Across Africa

African buyers in Ghana, Kenya, or Cameroon hesitate to buy from Nigerian vendors, and Nigerian businesses hesitate to wire funds across borders due to lack of a unified digital trust credential.
